Time-Series ML · Personal Research

Cycle-Phase Detection from Wearables

The challenge
The menstrual cycle shapes how athletes respond to training, but most people only know when their period starts. Watches record resting HR, HRV and breathing every night, and these shift after ovulation, but the signal is subtle and buried in noise from training, illness, alcohol and travel. The only labels available are period dates.
How it was solved
Mask the disrupted days, learn what each phase looks like from confidently labelled days, and use a hidden semi-Markov model that knows the luteal phase lasts about 13 days. It returns the most likely transition day and how confident it is, validated against hormone tests, with a live version that updates every day.
My part
A personal research project, built end to end: the data-collection platform, preprocessing, models, hormone validation and live detector.

An end-to-end, privacy-preserving platform that collects longitudinal HRV, resting HR, respiration, sleep, stress and training-load data from multiple participants, then models menstrual-cycle phases and detects the follicular→luteal transition (the wearable luteal onset) from continuous biosignals. Because resting HR and HRV respond to progesterone after ovulation, the estimate trails the hormonal LH surge by a few days.

  • Collection — a FastAPI web app plus a packaged cross-platform desktop app pull Garmin Connect metrics and push them to Google Drive via GitHub Actions, without ever storing credentials
  • Preprocessing — cycle-phase anchoring (early-follicular / late-luteal windows) and a Hampel-filter + Mahalanobis-distance confounder detector that masks illness, alcohol, travel and hard-training days without erasing normal cycle variation
  • Features — strictly causal, past-only rolling means, lag features and day-to-day deltas across HRV, resting HR, respiration, sleep stages & load

Models for the follicular→luteal boundary

  • Change-point baseline — an AUC-weighted composite of resting HR, HRV, respiration & sleep-stress that flags the follicular→luteal shift (out-of-fold AUC ≈ 0.85 for follicular vs luteal days)
  • Hidden semi-Markov model — a sequence model with an explicit luteal-duration prior (~13 days), giving a most-likely boundary plus a posterior over it
  • Live detection — a forward-filtered HSMM that updates P(transition has happened) each day using past data only; it beats a CUSUM detector on a cost that penalises early, late and missed detections

Key result · personal record

Averaged across 27 cycles and aligned to the estimated ovulation day (green line): resting HR steps up after ovulation while HRV drops down — a clean, statistically robust physiological signature. Click to enlarge.

External validation · mcPHASES (Fitbit + hormone assays)

Validated on the public mcPHASES dataset (42 participants with Fitbit data and urine hormone tests; ~90 confident cycles from 29 participants). The HSMM's estimated boundary lands within ±2 days of hormonally-confirmed luteal onset 65% of the time (median offset 0d, MAD 2.0d), and trails the earlier LH peak by a physiologically-expected ~3.5 days (MAD 1.5d). Offsets beyond −8 or +10 days are grouped into the end bins. Pooled emissions reproduce the same signature as my own record — resting HR & respiration up, HRV down, in the luteal phase.
